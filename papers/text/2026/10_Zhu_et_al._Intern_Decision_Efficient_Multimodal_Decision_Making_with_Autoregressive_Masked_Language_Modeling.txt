Interactive agents repeatedly choose among a finite set of actions, labels, or ordinal outcomes. Generating
a free-form response for each choice adds latency and complicates the interface between a model and its
environment. We present Intern-Decision, a family of 0.8B, 2B, and 4B multimodal decision models that return
structured choices and candidate probabilities from text and optional images. Our autoregressive masked
language modeling objective places one decision placeholder per field in a complete assistant JSON skeleton.
Ground-truth symbols appear only in supervision, while all fields are evaluated in a single causal forward
pass using the backbone’s native language-model head. The 4B model achieves a 90.02% unweighted average
accuracy across seven evaluation suites, compared with 88.74% for Jev v1.13.0. For a fixed 289-token request
with three fields on one RTX 4090, mean latency is 44.16ms for the 4B model and approximately 33–34ms for
the smaller models. On a separate 96-case known-distribution pilot, temperature scaling reduces expected
multiclass Brier from 0.628 to 0.550 and expected calibration error from 0.213 to 0.089. A confidence-based
handoff study on 111 public Jevbench-Hard items improves accuracy from 82/111 (73.87%) to 97/111
(87.39%) by routing 42 items to Atria-Dawn-Preview. We also describe qualitative game, visual interface, and
browser demonstrations. These results support compact decision models as a practical component of larger
agent systems.

1. Introduction
Driven by the rapid progress of large language models (LLMs), agent systems have evolved from passive
text generation models into interactive systems capable of perceiving environments, using tools, and taking
actions [19, 30, 32]. Unlike open-ended text generation, many agent operations are inherently closed-set
decision problems, where the model selects from a predefined set of alternatives, such as tool invocation, model
routing, and actions [20]. However, current agents often rely on autoregressive free-form generation even
when the underlying task only requires structured decisions, introducing additional decoding overhead and
complicating the interface between models and downstream controllers [19, 25, 30]. These observations
motivate the development of dedicated decision models that can directly produce structured outcomes together
with reliable confidence estimates for efficient agent execution.
Recentworkonstructureddecisionmodels, referredtoasSystem-1decisionmodels, exploresthisperspective
by replacing unrestricted generation with explicit decision interfaces and probabilistic outputs. Systems such as
Jev [27] provide typed interfaces for choice, score, and binary decisions, while several open implementations
investigate alternative architectures and inference strategies for efficient structured decision making [1, 17,
22, 26]. These interfaces primarily target textual decisions, and implementations differ in whether they score
multiple questions jointly or separately. For multimodal agents operating in dynamic environments, decision

models must additionally integrate visual observations, task instructions, and changing interface states, where
action selection depends jointly on visual evidence and contextual constraints [28, 29]. Moreover, practical
agent requests often require multiple decisions over a shared state, creating a need for efficient multi-field
decision models that can preserve the capabilities of pretrained multimodal language models while providing
structured outputs.
To address these challenges, we introduceIntern-Decision, a family of multimodal decision models based on
autoregressive masked language modeling. Our approach reformulates structured decision making as next-token
prediction over masked decision fields within a predefined assistant output schema. By retaining the pretrained
causal language model backbone and native language-model head, Intern-Decision evaluates multiple candidate
decisions in a single forward pass and produces both structured predictions and candidate probabilities without
iterative text generation. This design separates decision scoring from free-form response decoding, enabling
efficient inference while preserving compatibility with existing multimodal language models.
To adapt pretrained models to this decision-oriented interface, we construct a unified multimodal decision
corpus consisting of textual states, optional images, typed questions, and target decisions. The corpus combines
teacher-constructed challenging decisions with public task distributions, covering textual reasoning, instruction-
following decisions, visual judgments, interface actions, and interactive control scenarios. We instantiate
Intern-Decision with 0.8B, 2B, and 4B Qwen3.5 backbones [23], fine-tuning the language backbone while
keeping the vision tower and projector frozen. To improve the reliability of confidence estimates, we further
apply post-hoc temperature scaling [13] using a separate calibration split.
We evaluate Intern-Decision from three perspectives: decision accuracy, inference efficiency, and probability
quality. Across seven evaluation suites, including public Jevbench subsets [10], Intern-Decision-4B achieves a
90.02% unweighted mean accuracy, compared with 88.74% for Jev. For a fixed 289-token request containing
three decision fields, Intern-Decision-4B achieves a mean latency of 44.16ms on a single RTX 4090. We further
evaluate probability calibration through a separate known-distribution pilot and study confidence-based model
routing, in which requests are passed to a larger model when the decision model’s confidence falls below
a threshold. Qualitative demonstrations on visual interaction and browser assistance further illustrate how
compact decision models can serve as local decision modules within hierarchical agent systems.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• We introduceIntern-Decision, a family of compact multimodal decision models that formulate structured
decision making as next-token-aligned masked prediction, enabling multi-field candidate scoring with a
native language-model head in a single causal forward pass.
•We develop a unified multimodal decision framework, evaluate accuracy, latency, and calibration under
separate protocols, and illustrate visual interaction through qualitative demonstrations.
• We investigate confidence-based model handoff that passes low-confidence requests to a larger model,
and demonstrate how compact structured decision models can complement larger models in interactive
agent systems.
2. Related Work
Decision Models.Recent decision models target fast, structured judgments: given a state, questions, and
predefined answer options, they return typed decisions with probability estimates rather than free-form
text. Alongside TypeSafe’s Jev [27], which supports choice, score, and binary decisions and reports using
Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions (RLCD), related open implementations explore different
modeling and inference strategies for structured decision-making. JevK5 [1] adapts Qwen3.5 with LoRA and
derives candidate probabilities from next-token logits through a Jev-style interface. Kev [17] combines Qwen
backbones with LoRA adapters and a learned pointer head, providing self-hostable models and an API for typed
choices and scores. SemIf-OpenJev [26] provides a direct-scoring approach that derives option probabilities
from candidate-token logits of frozen language models, with optional shared-state prefill reuse across questions.
Laya [22] adopts an encoder-based, non-autoregressive architecture to produce choice, score, and yes/no
decisions in a single forward pass. Within this paradigm, Intern-Decision focuses on multimodal structured
decision-making, incorporating visual inputs alongside textual states and task instructions.

Autoregressive and Masked Prediction.Autoregressive language models such as GPT-3 [5] predict tokens
conditioned on a causal prefix, whereas BERT [8] reconstructs masked tokens using bidirectional context.
Hybrid approaches explore combinations of these paradigms: UniLMv2 [3] combines autoencoding and
partially autoregressive objectives through pseudo masking, while GLM [9] combines bidirectional encod-
ing of the corrupted input with autoregressive reconstruction of masked spans. Mask-Predict [12] predicts
masked target tokens in parallel and iteratively refines uncertain predictions. For classification, PET [24] uses
pattern–verbalizer pairs for cloze-based prediction and distills the resulting models into a sequence classifier,
while LM-BFF [11] performs prompt-based fine-tuning with label words and demonstrations, reusing the
pretrained masked-language-model head. Intern-Decision formulates structured decisions asautoregressive
masked language modeling, combining answer placeholders with next-token-aligned prediction in a causal
multimodal model. Its native language-model head scores candidates for all requested fields in a single forward
pass, without iterative refinement or answer feedback.
Calibration and selective computation.Temperature scaling fits a positive scalar to adjust predictive
confidence while preserving the most likely label [13]. Brier scoring evaluates the full predictive distribution [4],
complementing accuracy and binned calibration error. Confidence-based routing and cascading use uncertainty
to allocate requests between models with different costs [6, 7]. Our evaluation separates hard-label calibration
from an exact-distribution pilot and reports one escalation operating point rather than an optimized routing
policy.

A positive scalar𝑇 changes the probability spread without changing the argmax, apart from implementation-

Consequently, schema-conforming output does not depend on a free-text decoder generating valid JSON. It
also does not guarantee semantic correctness or agreement between related fields.
The service can return the probability ofyes for Boolean questions and an expected numeric value for
ordinal questions. These quantities differ from the discrete argmax label used for decision accuracy. For all
types, candidate probabilities and maximum probability remain available to downstream controllers.
3.5. Computational scope
The method removes iterative answer decoding and avoids separate field-by-field forward passes. It does not
eliminate the cost of processing the input: longer states, more image tokens, and larger schemas still increase
computation and memory. One request may yield multiple decisions, so request rate and decision rate are
different quantities. Causal ordering also means fields can have different contextual prefixes even when they
share a forward pass; the construction does not enforce permutation invariance or a jointly constrained output
distribution.

4.2. Calibration test set construction
This evaluation-only test set measures whether the
model assigns appropriate probabilities to all possible
outcomes of a stated random experiment. For example,
a fair die requires probability1/6 on each real face
and zero on impossible faces. Selecting one plausible
face does not establish a correct predictive distribution.
Similarly, the Monty Hall questions ask where the prize
is, with doors as outcomes; they do not ask whether
switching is the best action.
The set is constructed from 24 mechanism families
in six categories. Each family has two settings, which
vary numerical parameters or the observation protocol.
Each setting is emitted with canonical and reversed
option order, yielding6×4×2×2 = 96 cases, 16 per
category. The two order variants share the same state,
question, and reference distribution. There are therefore 48 paired settings rather than 96 independent trials.
Appendix C gives the family inventory and concrete input/reference examples.
For each setting, a deterministic Python generator specifies the sample space, priors, independence assump-
tions, and observation or selection mechanism. It computes the exact distribution using rational arithmetic:
counting and enumeration for finite outcomes, conditional-probability formulas for observed evidence, a
forward recursion for hidden states, and combinatorial or dynamic-programming calculations for sequential
processes. References are not Monte Carlo estimates or model-generated answers. Construction checks enforce
probabilities in[0,1], an exact sum of one, matching option keys, unique IDs, and equivalent reversed-order
pairs. Rebuilding from the archived generator reproduces the evaluated input and reference files byte for byte.
Inputs contain only the state and typed questions. Exact fractions, numerical reference probabilities𝑞𝑖,
derivations, and provenance remain in a separate reference file and are never sent to the model. The set is not
used to train the model, fit its temperature, or select a checkpoint. An exact-content check found no overlap

rather than measured saturated serving throughput. They do not imply the same frame rate for an interactive
agent, which also incurs observation, image preprocessing, action execution, and environment-update costs.
Batch size, warm-up, repetition count, timing boundaries, and Jev transport conditions are needed before
interpreting the ratios as controlled hardware speedups.

7. Conclusion
We introduce Intern-Decision, which makes finite multimodal choices through a single causal forward pass,
retaining the pretrained backbone’s native language-model head and exposing candidate probabilities through
a typed interface. The 4B model achieves the highest seven-suite average in the presented comparison, with
a 44.16ms mean for a fixed short request on an RTX 4090. Separate calibration and handoff studies show
how probabilistic outputs can support uncertainty analysis and selective use of a larger model. We believe
Intern-Decision can serve as a practical component of larger agent systems, but its generalization to arbitrarily
large schemas and complex environments remains an open problem.

C. Known-Distribution Test Construction and Cases
Mechanism coverage and reference construction
The pilot consists of six categories with four families each. Every family has two settings and every setting has
two option-order variants. The resulting 96 records cover the following mechanisms:
1. Direct randomness and support:uniform die faces with impossible options; weighted candy inventories;
deterministic single-color pens; rare winning lottery tickets.
2. Composed events and mixtures:sums of two dice; numbers of independent hits; independent backup
generators; delivery outcomes under an unobserved route mixture.
3. History, conditioning, and hidden state:independent coin flips after a streak; socks after known
removals; die outcomes given a restricted sensor observation; a two-state Markov process with noisy
sensor readings.
4. Dailyevidenceandobservationbias:defectscreeningwithastatedbaserate; conditionallyindependent
repeated tests; correlated copies of one test; waiting times observed at a uniformly sampled time.
5. Selective disclosure and puzzles:informed-host Monty Hall; uninformed-host Monty Hall conditioned
onasafereveal; prize-dependentswitchoffers; two-childproblemswithdifferentchild-selectionprotocols.
6. Sequentialandcombinatorialprocesses:distinctcouponscollectedafterfinitedraws; birthdaycollisions
on a toy calendar; gambler’s ruin; first-reward times with a guaranteed stopping limit.
The everyday descriptions define synthetic mechanisms with explicit parameters, not empirical frequencies
inferred from real-world populations. Their oracles use exact rational arithmetic: finite counting, Bayes’ rule
and total probability, hidden-state forward updates, coupon-count dynamic programming, birthday no-collision
products, gambler’s-ruin formulas, and truncated geometric probabilities. An input/reference pair carries a
stable ID and input hash. Validation verifies the option keys and probability support, exact normalization, and
unchanged references when the option insertion order is reversed. Reference metadata records the family,
setting, exact fractions, and derivation separately from the model-visible input.
